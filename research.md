# PaaniSamay – Research

## Problem in five lines
1. Place/water point: Public community water tap and pump station, Ward 4 Market Chowk.
2. Person affected: Daily household water collectors (homemakers and working residents relying on shared municipal water supply).
3. What I personally observed: On 18-11-2026 between 12:05 and 21:15 (20 recorded observations), queues grew up to 8 people and wait times reached 14 minutes during evening peak hours (16:40–19:30) as water flow dropped to Low, whereas off-peak afternoon hours had High flow and 2–3 minute waits.
4. Why it matters: Residents lose valuable daily time waiting in long lines during low-pressure evening hours because they lack visibility into when queues are shortest and water flow is strongest.
5. Proposed tool: compare locally observed queue and waiting-time patterns.

## Existing solutions
### Jal Jeevan Mission
Broad government programme for rural household drinking-water access and community participation in water management. It does not directly provide a small queue-time comparison tool for one shared water point.
Website: https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/

### WHO guidance for small drinking-water supplies
Guidance on improving management and safety of small drinking-water supplies. Its scope is broader than an individual queue-time recommendation tool.
Website: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088740

## Two sources actually read
Source 1:
Title: Jal Jeevan Mission – Har Ghar Jal (Department of Drinking Water & Sanitation)
Publisher: Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India
URL: https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/
Access date: 18-11-2026
1. The mission focuses on providing functional household tap connections (FHTCs) across rural areas to reduce the daily burden of fetching water from distant public standposts.
2. It emphasizes decentralized, community-owned water supply management through Village Water and Sanitation Committees (VWSCs / Pani Samitis) to ensure long-term service reliability.
3. While the programme tracks infrastructure coverage and water quantity at a macro level, it does not offer a micro-level tool for residents to compare hourly queue times at shared community taps.

Source 2:
Title: Guidelines for drinking-water quality: small water supplies
Publisher: World Health Organization (WHO)
URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088740
Access date: 18-11-2026
1. Small and community-managed water supplies frequently experience intermittent supply and fluctuating pressure, which directly impacts reliability and user convenience.
2. Effective management of small supplies requires practical, locally adapted monitoring routines rather than overly complex systems that local operators cannot sustain.
3. The guidelines focus primarily on water safety plans, sanitary inspections, and regulatory frameworks, leaving room for simple local tools that help communities manage collection schedules and reduce congestion.

## Fieldwork validation
Across 20 structured observations collected on 18-11-2026 from 12:05 to 21:15 (OBS-001 to OBS-020), the water pump remained operational (Working) throughout the day, yet service efficiency varied sharply by time slot. Between 12:05 and 16:20, water flow was predominantly High or Medium, keeping queue counts between 1 and 4 people and average wait times under 6 minutes. In contrast, during the evening window from 16:40 to 19:30, water flow dropped to Low and Medium while queue sizes surged to 4–8 people, pushing wait times up to 12–14 minutes. Conversations with waiting residents confirmed that most arrive after work or household chores without knowing that shifting collection slightly earlier (before 16:30) or later (after 20:00) cuts waiting time by more than half.

## Scope
The app analyses CSV records only. It is not live monitoring, does not test water quality and does not guarantee future queue conditions.
